## Do you need a permit for a shed in Brookhaven?

For homes in the unincorporated Town of Brookhaven, the Town's [shed brochure](https://www.brookhavenny.gov/729/Sheds-Brochure) says:

- A shed of **144 square feet or less** (12 by 12 feet, for example) in the **rear yard** doesn't need a building permit.
- A shed **larger than 144 square feet**, or one **outside the rear yard**, needs a permit.
- No shed can be larger than **600 square feet** or taller than **12 feet**.
- A permit-exempt shed has to stand at least **3 feet** from the rear and side property lines, and from the second street line on a corner lot. In a side yard it has to meet the zoning setbacks for your lot.

Rules change and every lot is different, so confirm with the Town before you buy: Building Division, (631) 451-8696.

### Live in a village?

Patchogue, Bellport and the other incorporated villages inside the town have their own building departments and codes, and the Town's shed rules don't apply there. In the Village of Patchogue, call the Building Department at Village Hall, (631) 475-4300.

### Other things to check

- **Your survey.** It shows where the property lines really are. Fences aren't always on the line.
- **HOA or deed restrictions**, if your neighborhood has them.
- **Power.** If you want lights or outlets in the shed, ask your building department what the electrical work needs.

## What a shed should sit on

The base matters more than the shed. A shed on soft or uneven ground settles and leans, the doors start to stick, and the floor stays damp and rots from below.

| Base | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| Compacted gravel pad | Most backyard sheds. Drains well and keeps the floor off wet ground. |
| Concrete slab | Large sheds, workshops and heavy equipment. Solid and permanent, but needs forms and curing time. |
| Paver pad | Sheds that should match a patio or walkway. Looks finished, and drains because it sits on a gravel base. |
| Blocks only | Small, light sheds on level ground. Cheapest, but blocks set on bare soil can sink unevenly. |

### How a gravel pad is built

1. **Mark out the pad** a little larger than the shed, so water dripping off the roof lands on gravel, not soil.
2. **Strip the sod and topsoil.** They hold water and compress.
3. **Level the area.** On a slope that means cutting into the high side, and sometimes a low block wall to hold the pad on the low side.
4. **Set a border** of timber or block to keep the gravel in place.
5. **Spread crushed stone in layers** and compact each one.
6. **Check level in both directions** before the shed goes on.

Around Patchogue the soil is mostly sandy and drains well, so a compacted gravel pad works for most yards. In a low spot that stays wet after rain, raise the pad or fix the drainage first. Our guide to [dry wells and French drains](/blog/dry-well-vs-french-drain-long-island) explains the options.

## Where to put it

- **Out of low spots** where water collects after a storm.
- **With room in front of the doors** to swing them open and roll a mower or wheelbarrow in.
- **With a clear path for delivery.** A prebuilt shed arrives on a truck and needs a wide enough route to reach the pad.
- **Where you can reach it in winter**, if it holds the snow blower.
