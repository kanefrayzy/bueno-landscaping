| Step | When | Why then |

These dates come from NOAA's 1991 to 2020 climate normals for Islip MacArthur Airport, the nearest official weather station to Patchogue. Treat them as a guide, not a deadline, and watch the forecast.

## Turning sprinklers off: winterizing

Water left in the pipes, valves and heads freezes and expands. That can crack pipe, split fittings and break the backflow preventer, and the damage usually doesn't show until the system is turned on in spring.

Winterizing usually means:

1. Shutting off the water to the irrigation system at its shut-off valve.
2. Setting the controller to off so it doesn't try to run zones over the winter.
3. Draining the backflow preventer and any exposed pipe above ground.
4. Blowing out each zone with compressed air, one zone at a time, until only mist comes out of the heads.

### Blowout or drain-down?

Some systems have manual or automatic drain valves. Draining helps, but water can stay in low spots of the pipe and inside the valves. Colorado State University Extension calls a compressed-air blowout the only reliable way to clear a system and warns against relying on drain valves alone ([their guide](https://extension.colostate.edu/topic-areas/yard-garden/home-sprinkler-systems-preparing-your-sprinkler-system-for-winter-4-719)).

A blowout needs care. Too much pressure damages pipe and heads, and compressed air should never be pushed through a backflow preventer. CSU's guidance gives limits of 80 psi for rigid PVC pipe and 50 psi for flexible polyethylene pipe. It also takes a compressor big enough to move a lot of air, which is why this is usually a job for someone with the right equipment.

### Is it too late to winterize?

Usually not, but don't wait. Underground pipe is protected by the soil, and one light frost rarely reaches it. The parts at risk first are above ground: the backflow preventer and the pipe leading to it.

If a hard freeze is in the forecast and your system still holds water, at least shut off the water and drain the backflow preventer, then schedule the blowout for the next mild day.

### What if it already froze?

Leave the water off and don't run the system to test it. Get the remaining water blown out so it can't freeze again, then let the spring start-up find the damage. Cracked pipe and valves show up as wet spots, low pressure or a zone that won't shut off, and they can be repaired before the season starts.

## Turning sprinklers on: spring start-up

Wait until hard freezes are unlikely. Turning on too early is how a system that made it through the winter gets damaged in April.

A spring start-up usually covers:

- **Opening the main valve slowly.** A sudden rush of water can slam pipes and fittings that have sat empty all winter.
- **Checking the backflow preventer** for leaks and damage.
- **Running every zone** and walking the yard: broken or buried heads, clogged nozzles, wet spots that point to a leak, and spray hitting the house, the driveway or the street.
- **Adjusting heads and setting the controller** for spring weather, not summer heat.

## Watering rules in Suffolk County

If your water comes from the Suffolk County Water Authority, its [odd/even schedule](https://www.scwa.com/oddeven/) applies:

- Homes with odd-numbered addresses water on odd-numbered days, and even-numbered addresses on even days.
- Automatic sprinklers shouldn't run between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m.

SCWA also points out that watering in the midday sun can lose up to 30% of the water to evaporation, and it offers an account credit for installing a smart controller that adjusts watering to the weather. Check [its site](https://www.scwa.com/water-quality/environment/lawn-garden-tips/) for current terms. If another provider supplies your water, check its rules.

## A simple yearly calendar

- **March:** check the controller, look for heads damaged by plows or shovels.
- **Late April to May:** spring start-up once hard freezes are unlikely.
- **June to August:** follow the odd/even schedule, water early in the morning, adjust for rain.
- **September:** cut back run times as nights cool.
- **Late October to mid-November:** winterize before the first hard freeze.
